Two things the calendar could not do: make a block, and make it come back.

## Draw a block straight onto the calendar

Press on empty grid where the work starts, pull to where it ends, let go. A dashed
ghost follows the drag, showing the span and the running length, and the block you drew
becomes the item's duration — no more creating something an hour long and then resizing
it.

A click that does not move still creates nothing, and double-click still drops a
default-length block, so nothing you already do has changed.

## Work that repeats

An item can recur, from the new **Repeats** field in the inspector:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Every day** | every day, weekends included |
| **Every weekday** | Monday to Friday |
| **Weekly on…** | the weekdays you tick — one, or several |
| **Monthly on…** | a date of the month, 1–31 |

A repeat is a property of the item rather than a pile of copies. One item owns the
whole series, so renaming it, recolouring it or changing its length changes every
occurrence at once, and a `.mgtodo` file stays one entry per piece of work however long
the series runs.

The item's scheduled time becomes the series' anchor: its **date** is the earliest day
an occurrence can land on — so turning a rule on never backfills the weeks behind it —
and its **time** is when they all start. An **until** date stops the series; leave it
blank and it runs on. A monthly rule on a date some months lack, the 31st, skips those
months rather than sliding to the nearest day.

Repeating blocks carry a ↻ and behave like any other, with one difference that follows
from being a series rather than a date: **dragging one moves the whole series.** Up and
down sets the time of day for every occurrence. Sideways retargets the rule where the
rule has a single day to retarget — a weekly series on one day moves to the day you
drop it on, a monthly one to that date. "Every day" and "every weekday" have no one day
to move, so they take the time and ignore the column.
